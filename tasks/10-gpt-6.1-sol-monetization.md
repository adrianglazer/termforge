## Product decision

- Free download, an explicitly started **seven-day trial with full functionality**, then a **US $14.99 one-time lifetime unlock**. No subscriptions, feature tiers, session-count upsells, or automatic charge at trial expiration.
- The latest user instruction selects dollars, superseding the pasted euro examples. Use the US storefront/base price of $14.99 and Apple's localized price points elsewhere; do not hardcode $14.99 globally or plan a price increase.
- No Termforge account, licensing backend, RevenueCat, or third-party payment service. Purchases belong to the customer's App Store account. Do not promise recovery across different Apple accounts.
- Positioning: “7 days free. Full access. Pay once. No subscription.” Define lifetime as a non-expiring unlock of Termforge, subject to Apple's refund/revocation handling; do not promise perpetual service, OS compatibility, or unrelated future products.

Apple's [App Review Guidelines, section 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) permit a free non-consumable trial and require a duration-based trial name and clear advance disclosures. Recheck current requirements during implementation. Do not substitute an auto-renewing subscription trial.

- Fetch products using `Product.products(for:)`; show StoreKit's localized `displayPrice` for the paid purchase. Missing products must produce a recoverable unavailable state, never a fake price or successful purchase.
- Accept only verified, expected-product transactions. Load access from `Transaction.currentEntitlements`, process `Transaction.updates` for the app lifetime, and finish handled transactions after delivery. Handle success, cancellation, pending approval, verification failure, and store/network errors distinctly.
- A verified, non-revoked lifetime entitlement wins over trial state. Refunds/revocations must remove that entitlement and recompute access; an independently valid trial can still apply.
- A free non-consumable remains owned after seven days; ownership alone does not imply an active trial. Compute expiration locally from the verified **original acquisition date**, not installation time, restore time, or the latest app launch. Inspect `originalPurchaseDate` versus `purchaseDate` semantics and prove restores cannot move the start forward. Use the original/earliest verified acquisition consistently.
- Trial duration is exactly `7 * 24 * 60 * 60` seconds; access expires when `now >= trialStart + duration`. Time zone and daylight-saving changes must not change it. A purchase cancellation or pending transaction does not start the trial.
- Offer Restore Purchases on onboarding, the paywall, and settings. Use `AppStore.sync()` only for an explicit restore action, then reload entitlements. Normal startup must not trigger repeated Apple sign-in prompts.

References: [StoreKit transactions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction), [transaction properties](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction-properties), [current entitlements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/currententitlements), and [explicit restore synchronization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/sync()).

## Access policy and user experience

Create one authoritative native access service, with a small testable state model exposed to the UI: loading/unknown, not started, active trial with expiration, expired, lifetime, and recoverable store/verification error. Purchase-in-progress and pending approval should not erase existing access.

1. Before starting the trial, explain its duration, full functionality, what stops afterward, the localized lifetime price, and that there is no automatic charge. Provide Start 7-day Trial, Buy Lifetime, Restore Purchases, Terms, Privacy, and Support. Buying immediately without a trial is allowed.
2. Start the trial through Apple's free product transaction. On verified completion, unlock everything and show a subtle trial status with a precise expiration date/time available in settings. Avoid repeated nagging.
3. During a valid trial and after lifetime purchase, expose the same complete feature set. Do not alter existing SSH/security policies.
4. After expiration, gate new SSH connections and every equivalent remote entry point: reconnect, duplicated/restored sessions, snippets that execute commands, SFTP connections/remote writes, forwarding, and jump-host routes. Enforce this in native connection/operation boundaries, not only by hiding a UI button or trusting a JS boolean.
5. Recheck on foreground, before remote work, at the foreground expiry boundary, and after entitlement updates. At expiry, stop further remote interaction and tear down live sessions, forwards, and transfers using existing cancellation/cleanup paths so an open session cannot bypass the trial indefinitely. Warn shortly before the boundary and explain interruptions. Preserve local drafts and handle partial transfers honestly; do not claim remote processes have been killed merely because SSH closed.
6. Keep saved hosts, settings, existing local data, and local draft recovery visible. Allow safe local copy/export, data deletion, support, and restore/purchase actions without paying. Never delete hosts, keys, files, or drafts because the trial expires, and never bypass existing authentication to expose protected data.
7. Purchase completion updates access immediately without an app restart. Lifetime owners must not see trial nags or a purchase demand just because product-price loading failed.

## Offline behavior and minimal abuse resistance

- Use StoreKit's locally available verified transaction state where possible. A previously verified lifetime purchase should work offline; product-catalog fetch failure alone must not revoke it. Apply genuine revocations when StoreKit makes them available.
- Offline trial use still expires at its original boundary. A fresh installation with no verifiable transaction state must request connectivity/restore rather than invent a trial or treat a transport failure as confirmed expiration.
- Do not use UserDefaults, SQLite flags, or a persisted `isPro` value as purchase authority. Reinstalling or restoring under the same App Store account must recover the original trial and not award another seven days.
- Add only proportionate clock-rollback detection: monotonic elapsed time while running and a protected last-observed time can prevent simple backward jumps from extending a known trial. Define a clear recovery path for suspicious clocks instead of permanently locking users out.
- Document the limits: a signed purchase date does not supply continuously trusted current time, and no-backend enforcement cannot guarantee resistance to device tampering or different Apple accounts. Do not build fingerprinting or a server solely to claim perfect prevention.
